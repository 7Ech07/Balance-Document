Scattergun

This change is meant to keep Scout in line with the other combat classes after the global explosive nerf. Now, he has to play a bit more aggressively which gives those classes more opportunity to land solid hits on him.

I'm also including a buff versus builings partly to help offset the other change and partly because the Scattergun's reliance on its uniquely high ramp-up for its damage makes it bad at fighting Sentry Guns to a degree I think is excessive.

  • Changes:
    • (+) Deals 15% increased damage to buildings
    • (-) Uses a new spread pattern with three rows of five pellets (15 total), 20% wider than the current shotgun spread pattern
      • The overall per-shot damage is identical; the damage is just split between more pellets

Force-a-Nature

The knockback on this weapon is often a liability. It makes mid-air combat more cumbersome, and the first shot blasts the target out of the second shot's effective range which makes two-shotting power classes less reliable. It is also annoying to play against. The knockback is an important part of the weapon's identity and playstyle, so rather than take a heavy-handed approach and remove it, I'm making slight adjustments to its properties and those of the weapon itself.

  • Goal: Make the weapon more reliable at dealing damage (and generally more user-friendly)
  • Changes:
    • (+) Increased bullets per shot to +45%
      • These extra five pellets form a + shape in the middle of the spread pattern, stacking on top of the existing pellets
    • (+) Self-knockback is now tied to an alt-fire attack that otherwise functions identically to the primary fire
    • (-) Damage penalty increased to 25% (max per-shot damage of 118)
    • (-) Knockback does not stun enemies, takes their existing momentum into account, and respects knockback resistances/immunities

Baby Face's Blaster

Players often complain that Boost is too easily lost. A couple of stray bullets or a bit of splash damage is enough to drop a Scout from 520 HU/s back down to 360 HU/s. The Boost drain on double jump compounds this by making it harder to dodge explosives.

Aside from this, I rather like the current design. It focuses on providing options to both Scout and the other team. It tries to control the power of the Boost by giving the other team the ability to slow the Scout down with damage, instead of with penaltiesto other parts of the gun. This means the Scout is still able to do normal Scout things instead of being locked out of jumping or being forced to chip people from range.

  • Goal: Refine the current design to be more user-friendly and consistent
  • Changes:
    • (+) Ratio of damage to Boost lost changed to 1:2 (was 1:4)
    • (+) Boost loss from double jumping changed to 50% (was 75%)
    • (+) User passively build Boost (at a rate of 2%/sec)
    • (+) Fall damage does not drain Boost
    • (-) Ratio of damage to boost gained changed to 3:2 (was 1:1)

Shortstop

The Shortstop has one of the slowest reloads in the game, which makes it feel cumbersome to use and extremely punishing when the user misses its very tight damage thresholds. The reload, however, does need to be slow because the Shortstop is capable of strong and consistent damage from outside the effective range of many other classes, so the Scout needs to be incentivised to take risks and secure kills in as few shots as possible to keep the weapon from feeling unfair.

Credit to Sile Animus for the segmented reload idea.

  • Goal: Add more forgiveness to the weapon without raising the mid/long-range DPS significantly
  • Note: This weapon is unaffected by the base Scattergun changes as it is not a Scattergun
  • Changes:
    • (~) Shells are reloaded one at a time instead of all at once
      • The initial animation of opening the weapon takes 0.2 seconds and each shell takes 0.35 seconds, for a maximum duration of 1.6 seconds
    • (-) Each shot is split into five pellets rather than four

Pretty Boy's Pocket Pistol

Following its rework, this weapon was overpowered because it had better DPS than the Pistol on top of its healing. I think the healing is the more interesting aspect of the weapon — the Winger also has a minor burst damage focus — so I'm going to shift the focus of the weapon entirely into that.

  • Goal: Remove the improved burst and tune the healing to better encourage aggression
  • Changes:
    • (+) Added: On kill, gain 15 health
    • (~) Healing on hit is now proportional to damage dealt (4 health at 19 damage down to 1 health at 10 damage)
    • (+) Removed: 15% faster firing speed

Bonk! Atomic Punch

Despite the re-addition of the slow effect when the invulnerability wears off, Bonk still doesn't require skill or carry much of a downside. The Scout is still able to disengage from a fight or rush past an entire team largely unimpeded, and since the slow only applies once the Scout has already gotten past the people shooting at him, most classes still struggle to chase him.

However, the amount of knockback Sentries do can leave the weapon feeling too easily countered when trying to push through nests, even though this is the intended main use of the item.

  • Goal: Tone down the more annoying aspects of the weapon and add more opportunities for counterplay, while improving its effectiveness against Sentry Guns
  • Changes:
    • (+) The Scout is able to freely swap weapons during the effect
    • (+) Knockback and damage (for the purposes of the slow) recieved from Sentry Guns is reduced by 66%
    • (~) Duration reduced to 5 seconds
    • (-) The slowdown from taking damage is applied during the phasing effect, rather than when phasing wears off
      • It lasts for 3 seconds and scales with the cumulative amount of damage recieved, and it can still linger once the phasing effect has worn off
    • (-) The Scout is no longer immune to melee damage during the effect
    • (-) Once the effect ends, the deploy animation of the active weapon must play before the Scout is able to attack

Crit-a-Cola

Mini-Crits are a strong effect, and the downside of the Scout taking Mini-Crits himself can be mitigated by a Medic, or simply exploiting his lack of fall-off to deal damage from a range where the opponent's attacks are easily avoided (particularly strong with the Shortstop). The Mini-Crits also don't improve Scout's ideal times-to-kill on non-Shortstop primaries, except against light classes who die in one shot, and I think this encourages trying to play more passively with the buff. While it may lose some of the flavour of the item, I think a different effect could help solve these problems.

  • Goal: Replace the Mini-Crit buff with an effect that encourages more aggression and leaves enemies more opportunity to hit the Scout
  • Changes:
    • (~) Instead of Mini-Crits, drinking grants 25% faster firing speed, 50% faster reload, the speed boost effect, and 35% increased bullet spread
    • (-) The Scout cannot be targeted by Medi-Gun beams during the buff
    • (-) Mark-for-Death is now applied immediately after drinking and lasts two seconds when the buff wears off, instead of being applied when attacking

Mad Milk

Thrown Milk jars can easily swing a teamfight because the debuff puts players at such a massive disadvantage while it's on them. I think the concept of debuffing enemies so that you can heal off of them is a bit silly, and if we unshackle ourselves from it we could have a version of the weapon that's easier to balance and isn't as frustrating to fight.

Credit to Sile Animus for the idea. We have playtested this and it feels quite good.

  • Goal: Shift the focus of the item from hindering enemies to directly helping allies
  • Changes:
    • (+) Teammates (including the Scout himself) in the blast radius heal 65 health
    • (-) No longer does anything to enemies
    • (-) Splash radius reduced to 150 Hu (was 200)
    • (-) Starts empty and is not refilled by the Resupply Cabinet

Flying Guillotine

Despite its 'recent' nerf, this weapon remains a powerful spam tool. Its high burst damage and short cooldown allow it to put out better ranged damage than the Pistol. I think the weapon should be reworked into an execution tool (it is a Guillotine, after all) that can finish wounded enemies better than the Pistols, but with lower sustained damage and a harsher downside for missing.

  • Goal: Reduce spammability down chokes and rework the weapon into a finisher that rewards deliberate throws
  • Changes:
    • (+) Added: Kills reduce recharge time by 50%
    • (+) Can now damage buildings
    • (-) Cooldown increased to 6 seconds (was 5.1)
    • (-) Removed: Bleed on hit
    • (-) Removed: Long-distance hits reduce recharge time
