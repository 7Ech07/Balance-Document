Pyro

Afterburn

In many cases, Afterburn is regarded as an annoyance rather than a threat. Locking a portion of Pyro's damage behind a slow DoT is one of the many things holding the class back, and is rarely fun or engaging to play against. If it were faster, it would give Pyro a bigger impact in fights, particularly if they can keep multiple people lit, while also not forcing opponents to endure it for as long.

  • Changes:
    • (~) Afterburn now deals 10 damage per tick for 3 seconds
      • Almost all Afterburn sources will apply the max duration of Afterburn, and it can never be extended beyond this duration (at least not in PvP)
    • (~) Applying Afterburn to a Pyro or other fireproof player target will make them count as being on fire for 0.75 seconds, but they will not take any damage from this

This is a change to the fundamental nature of Afterburn. It is focused less on scaring enemies into retreating for health and more on being a small but important source of direct-combat DPS that rewards the Pyro for juggling keeping crowds ignited and dealing direct weapon DPS. Note also that there is a trend in this document of making Afterburn harder to apply to justify its greater power.

Core class

Pyro struggles to stand up to the other combat classes. One of the reasons for this is that having a short range, stream damage primary forces them to have to expose themself to a lot of incoming damage in order to use it.

I don't expect or want this change to make it viable to just hold W at people. The aim is to make Pyro a bit less succeptible to chip damage, and help even the odds versus Scout and Heavy.

Flamethrower

In addition to being laden with jank, the Flamethrower has some fundamental flaws. Its ease-of-use significantly reduces the importance of the Pyro's aim and by extension the victim's movement, which leads to less interesting engagements. To account for this, the damage of the current version is rather lacking, with little room for the user's mechanical skill to improve it. Between this and their lack of mobility, Pyro struggles to get value from being proactive against better opponents and is often forced to sit and wait for the opponent to make a positioning mistake before being allowed to use their main form of attack. I don't want Pyro to become so mobile they overlap with Scout, but without any intrinsic mobility I feel they overlap too much with Heavy instead.

Pyro needs another rework; a good one this time! This section is rather long, and that's before even getting into Airblast specifically. You have been warned.

  • Goal: Increase the depth and skill requirement of the Flamethrower, and make it more fun and fair to fight
  • Changes (flames):
    • (+) Instead of being dependent on particle age, Flamethrower damage now has damage fall-off, linearly decreasing from 100% to 66% over 340 Hu
      • Because this is considered normal fall-off, buildings aren't affected by it and Crit damage bypasses it
    • (~) Flame damage now scales with the density of flame particles touching the victim rather than ramping up over time with sustained contact
    • (-) Max Flamethrower DPS reduced to 150 (was ~180)
    • (-) Targets will only become ignited after being in contact with flames for 0.5 seconds continuously
      • Flame damage dealt to an already-burning target will bypass this and reset the Afterburn duration to max
      • When a target is ignited, it will be accompanied by a particle and sound cue (like what the Dragon's Fury does on consecutive hits)
  • Changes (alt-fire):
    • (-) The Flamethrower has a Pressure meter with two charges; these act as a cooldown for Airblast and a new secondary attack
      • There is a 0.75 sec regen delay after using alt-fire, after which Pressure will start regenerating at a rate of 1 charge per 0.75 sec
    • (+) Added: Flame Blast attack – Hold M2 to charge a fireball and release to fire it, launching yourself backwards if airborne. Flame Burst requires and consumes a Pressure charge as well as the same ammo as an Airblast
      • The fireball takes 1.5 seconds to charge, and can be held for another 0.5 seconds before automatically discharging
      • Projectile speed scales from 950 to 2000 Hu/s and knockback scales from 50 to 250 Hu/s with charge
      • The fireball lasts 0.5 seconds or until it hits something
      • The fireball deals 20 damage, instantly applies Afterburn and applies minor knockback to burning enemies (a la the Dragon's Fury), and on surface hits destroys stickies and creates a lingering pool of fire for 2 seconds
      • Pressure is consumed on discharge instead of while charging, but Pressure will not regenerate while charging
      • If M2 is held for 0.2 seconds or less, it will Airblast on release; else, it will trigger a Flame Blast instead
Flamethrower

Flamethrower

Damage heats up flammable enemies until they catch fire, dealing additional damage over time.

Alt-fire tap: Discharge a blast of air that pushes enemies and deflects projectiles.

Alt-fire hold: Charge a blast of flame that burns enemies and terrain. Release to discharge, knocking you backwards if you are airborne. Projectile speed and knockback force increase the longer Flame Blast is charged.

Alt-fire requires Pressure, which recharges over time.

Airblast

The primary fire is only half the problem with the Flamethrower. Airblast is the other half.

While it gives Pyro something unique and situationally powerful, Airblast is almost a completely reactionary tool which doubles down on Pyro being a passive class. Pyro contributes a lot to defensive stacks (which I think are already too effective in Casual) but contributes little when playing against them. The use of reflects as Pyro’s main form of survivability and ability to gap close also leaves their matchup against Soldier very swingy and contribute nothing against the hitscan classes. For flames to be better, we need to redistribute some power away from Airblast.

  • Goal: Reduce the effectiveness of Airblast and Airblast-centric playstyles in favour of offensive play
  • Changes:
    • (+) Reflected projectiles use their own base damage when calculating self-damage rather than the Flamethrower's, and the Pyro has 50% damage resistance to their own reflected projectile explosions if no enemy is hit
    • (+) Reflected airborne stickies are converted to the Pyro's team affiliation, have their arm times reset and immediately detonate when armed, reflected stuck stickies are unable to be detonated while rolling, and other reflected projectiles have their fuses and lifetimes reset where applicable
    • (-) Airblast requires and consumes a Pressure charge
    • (-) Airblast knockback now respects knockback resistances and immunities (e.g. Heavy's intrinsic 50% resistance)
    • (-) Removed: Airblast reduces targets' air control
    • (-) Removed: Airblast extinguishes teammates

Dragon's Fury

Players sometimes complain about how this weapon feels inconsistent to use due to the variable repressurisation speed and need to hit with the centre of the projectile. As someone who spends perhaps an unhealthy amount of time using the weapon, I think the way it works now is entirely fine and even rather elegant. I don't want to compromise its strengths to make it easier to use or more like other weapons as quite a few people apparantly do.

What I think the weapon would benefit from is to hit important damage thresholds more reliably so it can better stand up to the other power classes even at higher levels of play. It could also do with minor tweaks to the Dragon's Fury v Flamethrower interaction, so the Fury isn't completely shut down by skilled Airblast use but isn't overbearingly powerful against less-skilled Pyros.

  • Goal: Minorly tweak the weapon's performance in various interactions, and address how the base Flamethrower changes would otherwise impact this weapon
  • Changes:
    • (+) Damage to non-burning players and on grazing hits increased to half of the damage to burning players (37.5 at base; was 25)
    • (+) Base repressurisation time reduced to 0.75 sec (was 0.8 sec)
    • (-) Projectile hitbox size reduced by 0.22...% (35 Hu in radius; was 45 Hu)
    • (-) Projectiles only apply 1 second of Afterburn, including on hits against already-burning targets
    • (i) This weapon no longer uses a custom Afterburn effect that damages Pyros, but Afterburn is applied for a guaranteed 0.75 seconds against Pyros due to the base Afterburn changes
      • This makes Pyros take an additional shot to kill at range compared to Soldiers, but also adds leeway for playing around opponents' Airblast
    • (i) Visual: Fireballs use simpler visual effects that do not have a dynamic light and which are actually attached to the fireball hitbox, instead of being separate and glitching out when the projectile is reflected or destroyed
