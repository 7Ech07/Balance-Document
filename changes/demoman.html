Demoman

Loch-n-Load

It's not a terrible weapon, but I've never particularly cared for the Loch-n-Load's design. It's not as interesing as the Direct Hit because grenades already require you to land direct hits. In practice, this is an easier-to-use Grenade Launcher, and I think it can be much cooler than that.

I decided to look back at the original design and try to come up with alternate ways to do burst damage without feeling like a simpler Loose Cannon.

  • New Design -- Rewards planning with burst damage
    • (+) On impact, grenades prime your stickybombs to explode with Mini-Crit damage
      • Stickies charge up over 0.3 seconds, and then explode; stickies outside the radius of the bomb are left untouched
      • Stickies can be detonated before their arm time is finished, but doing so imposes a 25% damage penalty, which cancels out the Mini-Crit damage bonus
    • (-) -50% clip size penalty
    • (-) Grenades shatter on impact

Loose Cannon

The Loose Cannon's knockback is sometimes counterproductive because it can knock enemies out of Double Donk range. It can also be frustrating to play agaainst because it can completely stun you in place and easily launches Heavies out of position. The weapon also generally suffers from a lack of consistency, particularly up close.

  • Changes: Make the weapon less annoying to play against, and add utility to compensate
    • (+) Added: 30% reduced damage to self
    • (~) Impact damage rescaled from 50-30 to 70-20 based on distance
    • (~) Building impacts deal 55 damage and cause the cannonball to lose all velocity
    • (~) Projectile speed and drag changed to match stock
    • (~) Knockback now scales with impact damage
      • Knockback strength is the same at point-blank, and scales down to 50% at max range
    • (-) Knockback no longer applies a stun or resets the victim's momentum, and now respects knockback resistances

Ali Baba's Wee Booties

The Booties strip Demoknight's options down significantly in favour of making him even more survivable, which I find annoying to play against in the situations where Demoknight is already good. Full Demoknight isn't very strong because it's tricky to balance an obligate melee attacker. As I said regarding Pyro, for a melee playstyle to be fun and effective "the user needs ways to leverage their gamesense and movement skill to get close to opponents with stronger ranged damage". I want the Booties to strive for that instead of trying to make melee W+M1 stronger.

  • New Design -
    • (+) Crouch while grounded to charge up a super jump over 1 second; jump while charged to perform a jump with triple height
    • (+) 50% faster shield recharge rate
    • (+) 25% knockback resistance

Stickybomb Launcher

Compared to a lot of other people, I'm mostly fine with the power of Demoman. However, I would like to introduce more counterplay to traps. Currently, traps have few ways to effectively destroy them and aren't telegraphed as well as many of the game's other one-shots.

I also think making stickies more obvious in general would make them more intuitive to play against. Rocket visuals make it clear where they are and where they're going, and they read as immediate threats. Stickies aren't as obvious and don't look immediately threatening even though they absolutely are.

Additionally note changes to how Airblast interacts with stickies here.

  • Goal: Increase counterplay to sticky traps
  • Changes:
    • (-) Stickies have a particle effect around them that shows their arm timer, and flash and beep periodically when armed
    • (-) Stickies can take damage from explosions, and are destroyed after taking 70 explosive damage
    • (-) Stickybomb hitbox dimensions are doubled (8×8×8 instead of 4×4×4)
      • This does not impact projectile physics (the model is used for that); it only makes stickies easier to shoot

  • Changes:
    • (+) Charging prepares up to 3 additional stickies at once

      • This weapon reloads at half the speed while holstered

Chargin' Targe and base Shield properties

Demoknight is very one-dimensional in his game plan. He goes in for an one-shot kill melee attack and then, if he doesn't die, he either runs at weakened enemies and holds M1 on them, or he runs away and waits for his charge to come back off cooldown so he can do it all again. He's a poor combat class and also an unengaging opponent -- either he comes in from nowhere and instakills you, or he gets kited or juggled to death without much effort. I think Demoknight has potential to be a strong, interesting and fun front-liner.

  • Goal: Take power from charging melee strikes and distribute it to the rest of Demoknight's kit
  • Changes:
    • (+) Added: While charging, shoot yourself in the feet with your primary weapon to perform a grenade jump
    • (+) Added: 25% reduced self-damage on wearer
    • (+) Added: Equipped Swords no longer have a deploy or holster speed penalty
    • (+) Charge recharges in 10 seconds (was 12)
    • (+) Shield bash hits, melee hits and kills with any weapon all restore 25% charge
    • (~) Base turn rate doubled but turn rate is decoupled from FPS
    • (~) Shield resistances are replaced by max health bonuses
      • On the Targe, this is +50 max health
    • (-) Charging no longer grants a full melee Crit

Swords

The Swords want to be at least on par with secondary weapons in terms of power on Demoknight, but it's tricky to make them so when they're still expected to be balanced around the other melee weapons. Some of this will necessarily come from the Shields, but leaving Demoknight reliant on the Shield charge for his power restricts him to just mediocre, boring melee W+M1 when it's on cooldown. I think, therefore, that the Swords could do with being a bit more interesting mechanically.

  • Goal: Add depth to the Sword archetype and increase raw effectiveness to compensate while keeping it in line on normal Demoman (and Soldier)
  • Changes:
    • (+) Added: Hold attack for 0.6 seconds to prepare a heavy thrust attack that has 100 base damage, 15% longer range, a 25% thinner hitbox and inflicts minor knockback
    • (+) Swords are able to benefit from melee ramp-up despite currently not having Random Crits

Scotsman's Skullcutter

The Skullcutter's identity is often considered being the only 'Sword' to retain Random Crits. This is not a particularly interesting identity, and the removal of Random Crits invalidates it anyway. I've come up with a way to retain a Crit gimmick on the weapon in the hopes of giving it a more well-defined purpose.

  • Changes:
    • (+) Added: Crits on third consecutive hit
      • Compared to the Gunslinger's current attribute, the window of time allowed between each hit is 50% longer
    • (+) Speed penalty while active only applies when not charging
    • (-) Added: No heavy attack
