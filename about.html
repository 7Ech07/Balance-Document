About Me

I started playing with Meet Your Match in 2016. This game has been a hyperfixation of mine since then. It was my first shooter and still easily my most-played. It is lightning in a bottle that may never be recaptured. I mained Spy for several years and then Pyro for several months, but I have no main anymore. My most played class is Pyro (largely because I find the Dragon's Fury uniquely fun) and my least played class is Engineer. Everyone else falls somewhere on this continuum. I have never played in a competitive league, but I like to think I've gotten decently good at the game. I almost entirely play Casual because everything else in Australia is dead. I play MvM (mostly community missions) occasionally and I like to think I'm decently good at that too.

I made this document for fun. It is an expression of my love for this game. I have no expectation of any of my ideas ever becoming official or even widely accepted by the community, but I am making this into a SourceMod plugin so I can properly experience my work and thereby refine it further.

My Philosophy

I think balance is important. Balance ensures fairness and expands the total pool of effective options available to players, both of which make the game fun. I think the game should be balanced around good players, as they know how to use weapons at (close to) their full potential and how to play against them too. Competitive formats are useful to gain insight into how certain things perform in certain situations in high-skill environments, but I’m not making any changes around them in particular.

I want weapons to promote fun gameplay interactions between players. This involves a couple of things:

    Agency: Players should feel like they always have some ability to influence what happens to them through their skill and decision-making — they should not have control stripped away from them, be punished unduly for the mistakes of other players, or be put into what feel like hopeless match-ups

    Skill: Weapons should encourage skillful play from their users, and should reward skill fairly; players should also be able to influence interactions using their skill, and they should rarely be put in situations where the outcome of an interaction feels binary or predetermined based on loadout or class choices
